## How it works

Enter how much you invest, your entry and exit price, your leverage (1 for a normal spot buy) and the fee your exchange charges per trade. The calculator gives you:

- **Profit or loss** in dollars after fees
- **Return on investment**, measured on the money you put in
- **Price move** between entry and exit
- **Position size** in coins
- **Fees paid** on the way in and out

<span class="formula">Profit = Quantity × (Exit − Entry) − Fees, for a long trade</span>

For a short trade the price difference is reversed: you profit when the exit is below the entry.

## Leverage cuts both ways

With 10x leverage, a 5% move in your favour becomes a 50% return on your margin, but a 5% move against you is a 50% loss, and a move of roughly 10% wipes out the position. The calculator shows an estimated liquidation price whenever leverage is above 1, and warns you when your exit price is beyond it. Use the [liquidation price calculator](../liquidation-price-calculator/) for more detail.

## Do not forget fees

Spot fees are often around 0.1% per trade, futures fees lower, but they apply to the full leveraged position. On a 10x position a 0.05% fee on each side costs 1% of your margin per round trip. Frequent trading with high leverage can lose money to fees alone.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is ROI in crypto?

Return on investment is your profit divided by the money you put in. A $1,000 investment that ends at $1,150 after fees has a 15% ROI.

### How do I calculate profit on a short trade?

Enter the price you sold at as the entry and the price you bought back at as the exit, and choose Short. A short from 60,000 to 57,000 on 1 BTC makes about $3,000 before fees.

### Does this include funding fees?

No. Perpetual futures also charge or pay a funding fee every few hours. Check the current funding rate on your exchange or in the Alice funding tracker.
