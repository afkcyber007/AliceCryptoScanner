## What is a pip?

A pip is the standard unit of price movement in forex. For most pairs it is the fourth decimal place, **0.0001**. For pairs that end in Japanese yen it is the second decimal, **0.01**. Many brokers quote one more digit, a fractional pip or "pipette", which is one tenth of a pip.

Gold and silver have no official pip, so brokers use their own convention. This calculator uses the most common one: **0.10** for gold (XAU/USD) with 100 ounces per lot, and **0.01** for silver (XAG/USD) with 5,000 ounces per lot.

## The pip value formula

<span class="formula">Pip value = Lot size × Contract size × Pip size</span>

The result is in the **quote currency**, the second currency of the pair. If your account is in a different currency, it is converted at the current exchange rate.

| Pair | Pip size | Pip value per standard lot |
|---|---|---|
| EUR/USD, GBP/USD, AUD/USD | 0.0001 | $10 |
| USD/JPY | 0.01 | ¥1,000 (about $6 to $7) |
| USD/CHF | 0.0001 | CHF 10 |
| EUR/GBP | 0.0001 | £10 |
| XAU/USD (gold) | 0.10 | $10 |

Whenever USD is the second currency and your account is in dollars, one pip on one standard lot is always $10. That is why EUR/USD and GBP/USD are the easiest pairs to size by hand.

## Why pip value changes

For pairs where the quote currency is not your account currency, the pip value moves with the exchange rate. When the yen weakens, a pip on USD/JPY is worth fewer dollars; when it strengthens, more. ## Frequently asked questions

### How much is 1 pip on 0.01 lots?

On a pair quoted in US dollars, such as EUR/USD, 0.01 lots (a micro lot) is worth $0.10 per pip. On other pairs it depends on the exchange rate; enter 0.01 in the lot size box to see it.

### What is the difference between a pip and a point?

Many platforms show five decimals on most pairs. The last digit is a point or pipette, one tenth of a pip. A move from 1.08500 to 1.08510 is 10 points but only 1 pip.

### How is a pip on gold calculated?

With the common 0.10 pip size and a 100-ounce contract, each pip is worth $10 per lot. A $1.00 move in the gold price is therefore 10 pips, or $100 per lot.
