Good trading days rarely happen by accident. Professional traders follow a routine: they prepare before the market moves, trade only during the hours that suit their strategy, and review their work at the end of the day. A routine removes guesswork and keeps emotions in check.

![A professional trading day in UTC: planning before London, trading the London and New York overlap, journaling after](img:edu/daily_routine.webp "A sample day in UTC: 15 minutes of planning, trading the London and New York overlap, 10 minutes of journaling.")

## 1. Pre-market preparation (15 to 30 minutes)

### Check the economic calendar

Look for high-impact events in your markets: central bank decisions, CPI inflation, non-farm payrolls, GDP. Note the times. Many traders avoid opening new positions in the 15 to 30 minutes before a major release.

### Read the higher-timeframe picture

For each market on your watchlist, check the daily and 4-hour charts. Is it trending up, down, or ranging? Write a one-line bias for each.

### Mark the key levels

Draw the previous day's high and low, the nearest support and resistance zones, and any round numbers. These are where reactions are most likely.

### Write the plan

For each market: "If price does X at level Y, I will look for Z." Include your maximum risk per trade and your daily loss limit.

## 2. Trade the active session

Every market has hours when it moves and hours when it drifts.

- **Forex:** the London session and the London–New York overlap (about 12:00 to 16:00 UTC) usually bring the most volume.
- **Gold:** the London open and the US session, especially around US data.
- **Crypto:** volume rises when Europe and the US are both active, roughly 13:00 to 20:00 UTC.

During the session, wait for your setups at your levels. Use alerts so you do not need to stare at every candle. When a setup appears, size the position from the stop distance before you enter.

## 3. Manage open trades

- Move the stop to break-even only at a planned point, such as after the first target.
- Do not add to losing positions.
- Respect your daily loss limit. If you hit it, close the platform.

## 4. End-of-day review (10 minutes)

Log every trade in your journal: the setup, entry, stop, target, result in R, and how you felt. Then ask three questions:

1. Did I follow my plan?
2. What did the market do that I did not expect?
3. What will I do differently tomorrow?

## 5. Weekly review

Once a week, look at your statistics. Which setups make money? Which sessions? Which emotions lead to losses? Adjust your plan based on data, not on one bad day.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many hours a day should I trade?

Quality matters more than hours. Many successful traders actively trade only two to four hours a day, during the most active session for their market.

### What if I have a full-time job?

Trade a higher timeframe such as the 4-hour or daily chart, prepare in the evening, and use alerts and limit orders so you do not need to watch the screen.

### Should I trade every day?

No. If there are no good setups or major news makes the market unpredictable, not trading is a valid decision.
