You can lose more trades than you win and still make money. You can also win most of your trades and lose money. The difference is the **risk to reward ratio**: how much you stand to gain compared with how much you risk on each trade.

![Trade plan with 1R risk and 2R and 3R take-profit targets](img:edu/risk_reward.webp "A trade planned in R: the stop is 1R away, the first target at 2R and the second at 3R.")

## The basic calculation

- **Risk** = the distance from your entry to your stop loss.
- **Reward** = the distance from your entry to your take profit.
- **Ratio** = reward ÷ risk.

Example: you buy gold at 2,650 with a stop at 2,640 and a target at 2,680. Risk is 10, reward is 30, so the ratio is 1:3.

## Thinking in R

Professional traders measure trades in **R**, where 1R is the amount risked. A trade that hits a 1:3 target makes +3R. A stop loss is −1R. This makes results comparable no matter the market or position size.

## Why the ratio and win rate belong together

The ratio alone means nothing without your win rate. The break-even win rate for any ratio is:

**Break-even win rate = 1 ÷ (1 + ratio)**

| Risk to reward | Break-even win rate |
|---|---|
| 1:1 | 50% |
| 1:1.5 | 40% |
| 1:2 | 33% |
| 1:3 | 25% |

With a 1:2 ratio you only need to win a third of your trades to break even. Anything above that is profit, before costs.

## Expectancy: the number that really matters

**Expectancy = (win rate × average win) − (loss rate × average loss)**

Example: you win 45% of trades at +2R and lose 55% at −1R. Expectancy = 0.45 × 2 − 0.55 × 1 = **+0.35R per trade**. Over 100 trades, that is +35R. If you risk 1% per trade, that is roughly +35% before costs.

## How to set realistic targets

A great ratio on paper is useless if the target is unrealistic. Place targets at real levels:

- The next support or resistance zone.
- A previous swing high or low.
- A Fibonacci extension, such as 1.272 or 1.618.
- A measured move from a chart pattern.

If the nearest realistic target gives less than about 1.5R, the trade is usually not worth taking.

## Managing the trade in stages

Many traders split the position:

1. Take half the position off at 2R.
2. Move the stop to break-even.
3. Let the rest run to 3R or trail it behind swing points.

This locks in profit while still giving the trade room to deliver a big winner.

## Common mistakes

- **Moving the stop further away** when price approaches it. That changes your ratio after the fact.
- **Closing winners too early** out of fear, which turns a 1:3 plan into a 1:0.8 result.
- **Forgetting costs.** Spreads and fees reduce your real reward, especially on short timeframes.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is a good risk to reward ratio?

1:2 or better is a common rule of thumb, but the right ratio depends on your strategy's win rate. Trend-following systems often aim for 1:3, while scalpers may accept 1:1.5 with a higher win rate.

### Should every trade have the same ratio?

No. Let the market decide: use real levels for targets and skip trades where the nearest target gives too little reward.

### Does a high ratio mean a better strategy?

Not necessarily. A 1:5 target that is hit only 10% of the time loses money. Always look at ratio and win rate together.
