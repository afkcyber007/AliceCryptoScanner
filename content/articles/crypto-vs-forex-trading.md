Forex and crypto are the two most popular markets for new traders, and many traders end up trading both. They share a lot, including charts, indicators and risk management, but they behave very differently. Here is an honest comparison.

## At a glance

| | Forex | Crypto |
|---|---|---|
| Trading hours | 24 hours, Monday to Friday | 24 hours, 7 days a week |
| Typical daily move | 0.5% to 1% on major pairs | 2% to 5% on Bitcoin, more on altcoins |
| Market size | The largest financial market in the world | Smaller, but very large in Bitcoin and Ether |
| Main drivers | Interest rates, central banks, economic data | Adoption, liquidity, risk appetite, crypto news |
| Leverage for retail | Limited in many regions (e.g. 30:1 in the EU) | Up to 100x or more on offshore exchanges |
| Where you trade | Regulated brokers | Crypto exchanges, regulation varies widely |
| Costs | Spreads and sometimes commission, overnight swaps | Trading fees, spreads, futures funding |

## Volatility

Crypto moves much more than forex. Bitcoin can move 5% in a day and smaller coins 20% or more. EUR/USD rarely moves more than 1% a day.

More movement means more opportunity and more risk. The same 1% account risk works in both markets, but in crypto your stops will be wider in percentage terms, so your position must be smaller.

## Trading hours

Crypto never closes, which suits people with irregular schedules but also tempts traders to watch charts at all hours. Forex closes at the weekend, which gives a natural break, but prices can gap on Sunday's open.

Both markets have clear busy and quiet periods. Forex is most active during the London and New York overlap; crypto volume also peaks during European and US hours.

## What drives each market

Forex is a macro market. Currencies move on interest rate expectations, inflation, jobs data and central bank decisions. If you like following the economy and the news calendar, forex rewards that.

Crypto is driven by flows and sentiment: ETF inflows, regulation, new projects, liquidations in the futures market, and general appetite for risk. Bitcoin is increasingly linked to US stocks and the dollar, so macro matters there too.

## Leverage and safety

Regulated forex brokers in many countries limit leverage and often offer negative balance protection. Crypto exchanges frequently offer extreme leverage, and the combination of high volatility and high leverage is the number one reason new crypto traders lose money quickly. If you trade crypto futures, use low leverage and always know your liquidation price.

Counterparty risk matters too. Choose a well-known broker or exchange, check its regulation and reputation, and do not leave more money on an exchange than you need.

## Which one should you choose?

**Forex may suit you if** you prefer steadier moves, like following economic news, want regulated brokers, and value a weekend break.

**Crypto may suit you if** you are comfortable with high volatility, want to trade at weekends, and are interested in the crypto space itself.

Many traders do best by mastering one market first, then adding the other. The skills are transferable: reading trends, levels and momentum, sizing positions and managing risk.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is crypto riskier than forex?

Generally yes, because of higher volatility, higher available leverage and less consistent regulation. Good risk management reduces the risk in both.

### Can I trade forex and crypto with the same strategy?

Many strategies, such as trend following and support and resistance trading, work in both. Adjust your stop distances and position sizes to each market's volatility.

### Which market is better for beginners?

Major forex pairs move more slowly and are easier to learn on. Crypto can be more exciting but punishes mistakes faster. Whichever you pick, start on a demo account or with very small size.
