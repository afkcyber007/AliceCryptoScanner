Moving averages are the most widely used indicator in trading, and for good reason. They smooth out noise, show the direction of the trend at a glance and act as dynamic support and resistance. Used well, they keep you on the right side of the market.

![EMA 21, 50 and 200 on a trending chart with a golden cross](img:art/ma_crossover.webp "The 21 EMA (gold) crosses above the 50 EMA (blue) while price holds above the 200 EMA (violet): a classic bullish alignment.")

## SMA vs EMA

- **Simple moving average (SMA):** the plain average of the last N closing prices. Every candle has equal weight.
- **Exponential moving average (EMA):** gives more weight to recent prices, so it reacts faster to new moves.

EMAs are popular with short-term traders because they turn sooner. SMAs are smoother and are often used for long-term levels such as the 200-day SMA.

## The most watched settings

| Average | Typical use |
|---|---|
| 9 / 10 EMA | Very short-term momentum, scalping |
| 21 EMA | Short-term trend and pullback entries |
| 50 EMA / SMA | Medium-term trend, institutional reference |
| 200 EMA / SMA | Long-term trend: above is bullish, below is bearish |

Because so many traders watch the 50 and 200, price often reacts at them. That is a self-fulfilling effect worth respecting.

## Reading the trend with moving averages

- **Strong uptrend:** price above the 21, the 21 above the 50, the 50 above the 200, all sloping up.
- **Strong downtrend:** the reverse.
- **Range:** averages flat and tangled, price crossing them constantly. In this condition, moving average signals fail often.

## Strategy 1: Pullback to the EMA

In a clear uptrend, wait for price to pull back to the 21 or 50 EMA. Enter when a bullish candle forms at the average, with the stop below the recent swing low. This keeps you trading with the trend at a better price.

## Strategy 2: The crossover

A **golden cross** happens when a faster average crosses above a slower one, such as the 50 crossing above the 200. A **death cross** is the opposite. Crossovers confirm trend changes but lag, so they work best on higher timeframes and with a filter such as the 200 EMA direction.

## Strategy 3: The 200 EMA filter

Only take long trades when price is above the 200 EMA and only shorts below it. This one rule removes many counter-trend mistakes from any strategy.

## Limitations

- Moving averages lag price. They confirm trends; they do not predict them.
- In ranging markets they give repeated false signals.
- No setting is magic. Consistency matters more than the exact number.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the best moving average for day trading?

The 9 and 21 EMA for timing and the 200 EMA as a trend filter is a popular combination on the 5- to 15-minute charts.

### Should I use EMA or SMA for the 200?

Both are widely watched. The difference is small; pick one and use it consistently.

### Do moving averages work in crypto?

Yes. Crypto trends strongly, which suits moving averages, but volatility means you should give stops extra room.
