Breakouts are exciting, but buying the moment price pokes through a level is one of the fastest ways to get trapped. The **break and retest** strategy waits for proof. After a level breaks, price often comes back to test it from the other side. If the old resistance now holds as support, you have a high-quality entry with a small, logical stop.

![Resistance becomes support after a break and retest](img:edu/sr_flip.webp "Resistance rejects price twice, breaks, and then holds as new support on the retest.")

## Why levels flip

A resistance level is an area where sellers have been active. When price finally breaks above it, two things happen:

- Traders who sold at resistance are now losing and want to buy back near their entry to get out.
- Traders who missed the breakout want a second chance to buy.

When price returns to the level, both groups buy. That demand turns old resistance into new support. The same logic works in reverse for broken support.

## The three steps

### 1. A clean break

Look for a strong candle that closes clearly beyond a well-tested level, ideally with above-average volume. A wick through the level is not a break.

### 2. The retest

Price pulls back to the broken level. It does not need to touch it exactly. Treat the level as a zone a few pips or a fraction of a percent wide.

### 3. The rejection

Wait for evidence that the level holds: a pin bar, an engulfing candle, or a lower-timeframe break of structure in your direction. That is your trigger.

## Trade plan

- **Entry:** after the rejection candle closes, or on a lower-timeframe trigger.
- **Stop loss:** on the other side of the zone, beyond the retest's wick.
- **Target:** the next major level. A 2R minimum makes the setup worthwhile.

## When to skip the trade

- **No retest at all.** Some breakouts never come back. Let them go. There will be another setup.
- **A deep retest.** If price falls back well inside the old range and closes there, the breakout failed.
- **Against the higher timeframe.** A 15-minute break and retest against a strong 4-hour trend is a low-quality trade.
- **Right before big news.** Volatility around releases can blow through any level.

## Combine it with other tools

- **Moving averages:** a retest that lines up with the 21 or 50 EMA is stronger.
- **Fibonacci:** retests often stop near the 38.2% or 50% retracement of the breakout move.
- **Volume:** falling volume on the pullback and rising volume on the bounce is the ideal picture.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long does a retest usually take?

Anything from a few candles to several days, depending on the timeframe. On the 1-hour chart, many retests happen within one or two sessions.

### Is the retest entry always better than the breakout entry?

It usually offers a better risk to reward and fewer false signals, but you will miss some strong moves that never pull back.

### Does break and retest work in crypto?

Yes. It works on crypto, forex, gold and stock indices. In crypto, check that the breakout came with real volume, because thin order books produce more fake moves.
