A **forex trading app** or **crypto trading app** can mean two different things: the app of your broker or exchange, where you place orders, and an **analysis app**, where you find and plan the trades. Most successful traders use both. Here is what to look for in each, so you can pick the right tools for 2026.

## Broker and exchange apps: where you trade

Your broker or exchange app should give you:

- **Fast, reliable order execution** with stop loss and take profit on every order.
- **Low costs:** tight spreads and low commissions on forex, low maker and taker fees on crypto.
- **Security:** two-factor authentication, withdrawal protection and a strong track record.
- **Easy deposits and withdrawals** in your country.

See our comparison of the best forex brokers and crypto exchanges for fees, platforms and leverage.

## Analysis apps: where you find the trade

A good analysis app saves you hours. Look for:

1. **A market scanner** that covers crypto, forex, gold, indices and stocks on every timeframe.
2. **Clear charts** with support and resistance, Fibonacci and indicators.
3. **Alerts** for price, RSI, breakouts and volume, delivered to your phone.
4. **News and an economic calendar** with the high-impact events marked.
5. **Risk tools:** position size, pip value, margin and liquidation calculators.
6. **Learning:** lessons and explanations, not just signals.
7. **One account everywhere:** phone, tablet and computer.

## Forex app for beginners: what matters most

If you are new, choose an app that teaches as it shows. The best **forex app for beginners** explains why a market is bullish or bearish, shows the trading sessions in your time zone, and has a risk calculator built in so you never trade without a stop loss. Practise on a demo account before you trade real money.

## Crypto alerts and whale tracking

Crypto moves 24/7, so a **crypto alerts app** is essential. Useful alerts include price levels, RSI extremes, volume spikes, new listings and large whale transactions. Push notifications let you react in time without staring at the chart.

## Free vs paid trading apps

Many apps are free with ads and a limited set of markets or timeframes, and paid plans unlock everything. A good free plan should still be useful on its own: a real scanner, alerts and calculators. Pay when the extra markets, timeframes or tools will clearly save you time or improve your trades.
