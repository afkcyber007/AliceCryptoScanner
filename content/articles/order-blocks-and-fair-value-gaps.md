**Smart Money Concepts (SMC)** is one of the most searched trading topics of the last few years. It describes how large players (banks, funds and market makers) leave footprints on the chart: zones where they placed big orders, gaps where price moved too fast, and pools of stop losses they use for liquidity. This guide explains the core tools, **order blocks** and **fair value gaps**, and turns them into a strategy you can test.

![Order block, fair value gap and break of structure on a chart](img:art/order_block_fvg.webp "After a break of structure, price often returns to the fair value gap or the order block before continuing.")

## Market structure first: BOS and CHoCH

Everything in SMC starts with **market structure**:

- **Break of structure (BOS):** price breaks the last swing high in an uptrend (or the last swing low in a downtrend). The trend continues.
- **Change of character (CHoCH):** the first break against the trend. In a downtrend, the first break of a lower high is a CHoCH and a warning that buyers may be taking over.

Trade in the direction of the latest BOS on your higher timeframe. A CHoCH on a lower timeframe is often the trigger to enter in that direction.

## What is an order block?

An **order block** is the last opposite candle (or small group of candles) before a strong move that breaks structure.

- **Bullish order block:** the last down candle before a strong move up.
- **Bearish order block:** the last up candle before a strong move down.

The idea is that large orders were filled there, and some were left unfilled. When price returns to the zone, those orders can push it away again. Good order blocks have three things in common: they caused a **break of structure**, the move away was **strong**, and they have **not been revisited** yet.

## What is a fair value gap?

A **fair value gap (FVG)** is a three-candle pattern where the middle candle moves so fast that the wicks of the first and third candles do not overlap. That gap shows an imbalance: one side overwhelmed the other. Price often comes back to "fill" part of the gap before continuing. In **FVG trading**, the middle of the gap (the 50% level) is a popular entry point.

## Liquidity: why price sweeps highs and lows

Stop losses cluster above equal highs and below equal lows. Large players need that liquidity to fill big orders, so price often runs through those levels (a **liquidity sweep**) and then reverses sharply. In SMC, a sweep of a high or low followed by a CHoCH is one of the strongest reversal signals.

![Liquidity sweep below equal lows](img:edu/liquidity_sweep.webp "Equal lows attract stop losses. A quick sweep below them followed by a strong close back inside often marks the turn.")

## The smart money concepts strategy in five steps

1. **Higher timeframe bias (4H or daily):** find the latest BOS. Only trade in that direction.
2. **Point of interest:** mark the fresh order block or fair value gap that caused the last BOS.
3. **Wait for price to return** to that zone. Do not chase.
4. **Lower timeframe confirmation (5M to 15M):** a liquidity sweep and a CHoCH in your direction inside the zone.
5. **Entry, stop and target:** enter on the lower timeframe order block or FVG, stop beyond the zone, target the next liquidity (the opposite highs or lows). Aim for at least 1:3 risk reward.

## Common SMC mistakes

- **Marking every candle as an order block.** Only zones that broke structure count.
- **Ignoring the higher timeframe.** A perfect 5-minute setup against the daily trend often fails.
- **No stop loss because "the zone will hold".** Zones fail all the time. Size every trade from the stop.
- **Trading during big news.** CPI and Fed decisions can wipe through any zone.

## Do order blocks really work?

Order blocks and fair value gaps are a structured way of trading **supply and demand** and momentum, ideas that have existed for decades. They work best combined with trend, liquidity and risk management, and like any method they need testing. Backtest at least 50 trades on your market before using real money.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the difference between an order block and a fair value gap?

An order block is the last opposite candle before a strong move; a fair value gap is the price gap created by that strong move. They often sit next to each other and form one area of interest.

### Which timeframe is best for SMC trading?

Use the 4-hour or daily chart for bias and zones, and the 5-minute to 15-minute chart for entries.

### Is SMC the same as ICT?

Many SMC ideas come from ICT (Inner Circle Trader) concepts. The terms overlap: order blocks, fair value gaps, liquidity, BOS and CHoCH.

### Can I use SMC on crypto and gold?

Yes. SMC is popular on Bitcoin, gold (XAU/USD) and the major forex pairs, which all have deep liquidity.
