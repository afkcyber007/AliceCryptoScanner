Most traders know what they think they do. A trading journal shows what they actually do. It is the cheapest and most effective way to improve, because it turns vague feelings like "I always lose on Fridays" into facts you can act on.

## Why a journal works

Memory is selective. We remember the big win and the painful loss, and forget the forty ordinary trades that really decide our results. A journal records every trade the same way, so after a few weeks you can see:

- which setups make money and which do not
- which markets and hours suit you
- whether you follow your own rules
- how emotions change your decisions

## What to record for every trade

| Field | Example |
|---|---|
| Date and time | 14 Oct, 14:35 UTC |
| Market and direction | EUR/USD, long |
| Setup | Break and retest of 4H resistance |
| Entry, stop, target | 1.0852, 1.0828, 1.0900 |
| Risk | 1% ($50) |
| Result | +$98, or +1.96R |
| Followed the plan? | Yes / no, and why |
| Emotion | Calm, rushed, frustrated |
| Screenshot | Chart at entry and at exit |

The **R multiple** is the most useful number: the result divided by the amount you risked. A win of $98 on a $50 risk is +1.96R. A full loss is −1R. R lets you compare trades of different sizes and markets.

## The statistics that matter

After 30 to 50 trades, calculate:

- **Win rate**: winning trades divided by all trades.
- **Average win and average loss**, in R.
- **Expectancy**: the average result per trade.

<span class="formula">Expectancy = Win rate × Average win − Loss rate × Average loss</span>

A positive expectancy means your method makes money on average. If it is negative, no amount of discipline will fix it; the setup itself needs to change.

Break the statistics down by **setup**, **market**, **day of the week** and **time of day**. That is usually where the biggest insights are hiding.

## How to review your journal

- **Daily, two minutes:** did I follow my rules today?
- **Weekly, twenty minutes:** look at all trades, update the statistics, write down one thing to keep and one thing to change.
- **Monthly:** compare setups and markets, cut what does not work, and focus on what does.

Be honest in the "followed the plan" column. A winning trade that broke your rules is a bad trade; a losing trade that followed them is a good one.

## Common journaling mistakes

- Only recording losing trades, or only winning ones.
- Recording results without the setup, so you cannot learn which setups work.
- Collecting data and never reviewing it.
- Changing the method every week, so no setup ever has enough trades to judge.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many trades do I need before the statistics mean anything?

At least 30, and ideally 50 to 100 trades of the same setup, before you draw firm conclusions.

### Should I journal demo trades?

Yes. A demo journal is the best way to test a new setup before risking real money.

### What is a good win rate?

It depends on your risk to reward. A 35% win rate is excellent with 1:3 trades, and a 60% win rate can still lose money with poor risk to reward.
