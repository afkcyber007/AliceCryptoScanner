Most traders do not fail because their strategy is bad. They fail because they do not follow it. They move stops, double down after a loss, chase a move they missed, or risk too much after a winning streak. **Trading psychology** is the skill of doing the right thing even when your emotions are shouting at you to do the opposite.

![Equity curves for 1%, 2% and 5% risk per trade with the same strategy](img:edu/equity_curves.webp "Same strategy, same 200 trades, different risk per trade. Higher risk means far bigger swings, and a far higher chance of blowing up.")

## The four emotions that cost the most

### Fear

Fear makes you close winners too early, skip valid setups after a loss, and hesitate at entries. The cure is a plan you trust and a position size small enough that a loss does not hurt.

### Greed

Greed makes you take profits too late, add to positions without a plan and risk too much. It peaks after a winning streak, which is exactly when discipline matters most.

### FOMO

The fear of missing out makes you chase a candle that has already moved. Chasing usually means entering at a poor price with a wide stop. Remember: there is always another trade.

### Revenge

After a loss, the urge to "win it back" leads to bigger size, worse setups and more losses. Revenge trading turns a small losing day into a disaster.

## Risk per trade decides whether you survive

The chart above shows the same strategy traded with 1%, 2% and 5% risk per trade. With higher risk, the account grows faster in good streaks, but the drawdowns are huge, and a normal losing streak can destroy it. With 1% risk, ten losses in a row cost about 10%. With 5% risk, the same streak costs about 40%, and you need a 67% gain just to get back to where you started.

## Ten rules that build discipline

1. **Write your plan before the session.** Bias, levels, setups and maximum loss for the day.
2. **Risk a fixed percentage** of your account per trade, usually 0.5% to 1%.
3. **Set a daily loss limit.** Stop trading after two or three losses, or after losing 2% to 3% in a day.
4. **Never move the stop loss away from price.** You can move it closer to lock in profit, never further.
5. **Only trade your setups.** If it is not in your plan, it is not a trade.
6. **Use alerts instead of staring at charts.** Watching every tick creates impulsive trades.
7. **Take a break after a big win or a big loss.** Both distort your judgement.
8. **Keep a journal,** including how you felt before and after each trade.
9. **Review weekly.** Look for patterns: which setups, sessions and emotions lead to losses?
10. **Think in a series of trades.** One trade means nothing. The next 100 trades are what count.

## Why a journal is your best psychology tool

When you log each trade with the setup, the result in R and your emotion, the patterns become obvious: maybe your revenge trades lose 80% of the time, or your Friday afternoon trades are all losers. Data turns vague feelings into clear rules.

## Consistency compounds

Small, consistent gains grow an account far more reliably than occasional big wins. A steady 2% to 3% a month compounds into a meaningful result over a few years, without the risk of ruin.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I stop revenge trading?

Set a hard daily loss limit and walk away when you hit it. Make it a rule, not a decision you take in the moment.

### Is it normal to feel nervous when trading?

Yes. If you feel very nervous, your position size is probably too large. Reduce it until a loss feels like a normal business cost.

### How long does it take to become a disciplined trader?

It is a habit, so it builds with repetition. Following a written plan and reviewing your journal every week speeds it up a lot.
