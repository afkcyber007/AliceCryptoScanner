Price tells you where the market is. Momentum tells you how hard it is pushing. When the two disagree, you have a **divergence**, and it is one of the earliest warnings that a trend is getting tired. The Relative Strength Index (RSI) is the most popular tool for spotting it.

![Bullish RSI divergence: price makes a lower low while RSI makes a higher low](img:edu/rsi_divergence.webp "Bullish divergence: price makes a lower low, but RSI makes a higher low. Sellers are losing strength.")

## Regular divergence: the reversal warning

### Bullish divergence

Price makes a **lower low**, but RSI makes a **higher low**. The market fell further, yet the selling pressure behind the move was weaker. It often appears near the end of a downtrend.

### Bearish divergence

Price makes a **higher high**, but RSI makes a **lower high**. Buyers pushed to a new high with less force than before. It often appears near the end of an uptrend.

## Hidden divergence: the continuation signal

Hidden divergence points the other way. It shows a trend that is pausing, not ending.

- **Hidden bullish:** price makes a higher low while RSI makes a lower low. In an uptrend, this pullback is a buying opportunity.
- **Hidden bearish:** price makes a lower high while RSI makes a higher high. In a downtrend, it is a selling opportunity.

## How to find divergence correctly

1. **Compare swing points, not random candles.** Connect two clear swing lows (or highs) on price and the matching points on RSI.
2. **Use closes for RSI.** RSI is calculated from closing prices, so compare it with candle closes or clear wicks consistently.
3. **Keep it recent.** Divergence between two swings that are 100 candles apart is not meaningful. Five to forty candles is typical.
4. **Prefer extreme zones.** Bullish divergence with the first low below 30, or bearish divergence with the first high above 70, is more significant.

## Divergence is a warning, not an entry

The biggest mistake is selling the moment you see bearish divergence. Strong trends can show divergence several times before they turn. Wait for the price to confirm:

- **A break of structure:** for bullish divergence, a break above the last lower high.
- **A key level:** divergence at a daily support or resistance zone carries more weight.
- **A candlestick signal:** an engulfing candle or a pin bar at the second swing.

### A simple plan

- **Entry:** after the confirmation candle closes.
- **Stop:** beyond the second swing (below the lower low for bullish divergence).
- **Targets:** the previous swing high for the first target, then a 2R or 3R target.

## Divergence on different markets

- **Forex:** works well on the 1-hour to daily charts, especially at session highs and lows.
- **Gold:** divergence at round numbers like 2,600 or 2,700 is worth watching closely.
- **Crypto:** strong trends can stay "overbought" for a long time. Combine divergence with funding rates and open interest for better timing.

## Frequently asked questions

### Which RSI settings are best for divergence?

The standard 14-period RSI works for most traders. Shorter settings produce more signals but more noise.

### Can divergence fail?

Yes, often. In a strong trend, momentum can stay weak while price keeps going. That is why you wait for a break of structure and always use a stop loss.

### Is MACD divergence better than RSI divergence?

They measure momentum in slightly different ways. When both show divergence at the same time, the signal is stronger.
