There is no single best way to trade. The right style depends on how much time you have, how you handle stress and which markets you trade. Choosing a style that fits your life is one of the most important decisions you will make, because a strategy you cannot follow consistently will not work for you.

![Trading styles by timeframe and holding period](img:art/trading_styles.webp "From seconds to weeks: scalping, day trading, swing trading and position trading use different timeframes and holding periods.")

## The four main styles

| Style | Chart timeframes | Holding time | Trades per week | Screen time |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Scalping | 1M to 5M | Seconds to minutes | 20 to 100+ | High, focused |
| Day trading | 5M to 1H | Minutes to hours | 5 to 20 | 2 to 4 hours a day |
| Swing trading | 1H to 1D | Days to weeks | 1 to 5 | 30 to 60 minutes a day |
| Position trading | 1D to 1W | Weeks to months | A few a month | A few hours a week |

## Scalping

Scalpers aim for small moves many times a day. They need tight spreads, fast execution and intense focus.

- **Pros:** many opportunities, no overnight risk, quick feedback.
- **Cons:** costs add up fast, very stressful, mistakes are frequent.
- **Best for:** experienced traders with low-cost accounts who can focus for long periods.

## Day trading

Day traders open and close positions within the day, usually during the most active session.

- **Pros:** no overnight risk, clear daily routine, enough setups.
- **Cons:** needs several hours of free time during market hours.
- **Best for:** traders who can commit to a fixed session, such as London or New York.

## Swing trading

Swing traders capture moves that last several days, using the 4-hour and daily charts.

- **Pros:** fits around a full-time job, fewer and higher-quality trades, lower costs relative to profit.
- **Cons:** overnight and weekend risk, needs patience.
- **Best for:** most beginners and anyone with limited time.

## Position trading

Position traders hold for weeks or months, following major trends and fundamentals.

- **Pros:** very little screen time, the biggest moves per trade.
- **Cons:** wide stops require small positions; long drawdowns test patience.

## How to choose

Ask yourself:

1. **How much time do I really have?** Be honest. If it is one hour in the evening, swing trading is the natural fit.
2. **How do I handle stress?** Fast decisions under pressure suit scalpers. Slower, planned decisions suit swing traders.
3. **What are my costs?** High spreads or fees make scalping almost impossible to profit from.
4. **What is my personality?** Patient people often prefer swing trading; action-oriented people lean toward day trading.

## The style changes the numbers

Shorter timeframes mean smaller stops and targets, so spreads take a bigger share of every trade. A 1-pip spread is 20% of a 5-pip scalp but only 2% of a 50-pip swing. That is why many beginners do better on higher timeframes.

## Frequently asked questions

### Which trading style is best for beginners?

Swing trading usually is: it gives time to think, has lower costs per trade and fits around work.

### Can I combine styles?

Yes, but keep separate rules and statistics for each style so you know what actually works.

### Is scalping profitable?

It can be for a small number of disciplined traders with very low costs. For most beginners, costs and stress make it the hardest style.
