The MACD (Moving Average Convergence Divergence) combines trend and momentum in one indicator. It shows whether a move is gaining or losing strength, which makes it useful for confirming trends and spotting turning points.

![MACD line, signal line and histogram below a price chart](img:art/macd.webp "Top: price. Bottom: the MACD line (gold), signal line (blue) and histogram. Crossovers and the zero line mark shifts in momentum.")

## How MACD is built

With the standard settings 12, 26 and 9:

- **MACD line** = 12-period EMA − 26-period EMA.
- **Signal line** = 9-period EMA of the MACD line.
- **Histogram** = MACD line − signal line.

When the fast average pulls away from the slow one, the MACD line moves away from zero. When they converge, it moves back toward zero.

## Reading the three parts

- **Above zero:** the short-term average is above the long-term average. Momentum favours buyers.
- **Below zero:** sellers have the edge.
- **Histogram growing:** momentum is accelerating.
- **Histogram shrinking:** momentum is fading, often before price turns.

## Strategy 1: Signal line crossover

A bullish signal comes when the MACD line crosses above the signal line; a bearish signal when it crosses below. On their own these crossovers are frequent and noisy. They work best:

- In the direction of the higher-timeframe trend.
- When the bullish crossover happens below zero (in a pullback) inside an uptrend.

## Strategy 2: Zero-line cross

When the MACD line crosses above zero, the 12 EMA has crossed above the 26 EMA. It is a slower but more reliable trend signal, useful for confirming that a new trend has started.

## Strategy 3: MACD divergence

If price makes a higher high but the MACD makes a lower high, upward momentum is fading. If price makes a lower low and the MACD a higher low, selling is weakening. Treat divergence as a warning, and wait for a break of structure before entering.

## Settings for different styles

- **Standard (12, 26, 9):** works on most timeframes.
- **Faster (8, 17, 9):** reacts quicker, for short-term trading, with more false signals.
- **Slower (19, 39, 9):** fewer, smoother signals for swing traders.

## Limitations

MACD is based on moving averages, so it lags. In tight ranges it produces many whipsaws. Combine it with support and resistance and the higher-timeframe trend rather than trading every crossover.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is MACD better than RSI?

They answer different questions. RSI shows overbought and oversold conditions, while MACD shows the strength and direction of momentum. Many traders use both.

### What timeframe is best for MACD?

It works on all timeframes, but signals on the 1-hour, 4-hour and daily charts are more reliable than on very short charts.

### Should I change the default settings?

Only if you have tested the change. The defaults are what most traders watch, which helps.
