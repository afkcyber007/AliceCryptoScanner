A trading signal looks simple: a pair, a direction and a few numbers. But two traders can copy the same signal and end the week with very different results, because one knows how to read it and the other does not. This guide explains every line of a signal and how professionals manage it from entry to the last target.

## The parts of a trading signal

A complete signal always answers four questions: **what**, **which direction**, **where to get out if wrong** and **where to take profit**.

| Line | Example | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| Pair | XAU/USD | The market to trade |
| Side | BUY / LONG | Buy now, sell later (SELL or SHORT is the opposite) |
| Entry | 4,012.50 | The price to open the trade, or a small zone around it |
| Stop loss (SL) | 4,001.00 | Where the trade is closed at a small loss if the idea fails |
| TP1, TP2, TP3… | 4,018 / 4,025 / 4,040 | Take-profit levels, from the closest to the furthest |

A signal without a stop loss is not a signal. It is a hope. Never trade one.

## Why signals have several targets

Markets rarely move in a straight line. A signal with only one far target often watches price come close, turn around and hit the stop. Several targets solve that problem:

- **TP1** is close. It is hit most often and pays for the trade quickly.
- **TP2** is the main target, usually around one to two times the risk.
- **TP3, TP4, TP5** are runners. They catch the big moves that happen a few times a month.

In crypto futures signals you often see five targets, for example +2%, +5%, +8%, +13% and +23% on price. In forex signals targets are usually written in pips, for example +20, +50 and +100 pips.

## How to manage the trade, step by step

1. **Size the position from the stop.** Decide how much of your account you are willing to lose if the stop is hit, often 1%. Then calculate the lot size or contract size so the distance to the stop equals that amount.
2. **Split the position.** A simple plan is to close one third at TP1, one third at TP2 and let the rest run.
3. **Move the stop to entry after TP2.** From that moment the trade can no longer lose money. Many VIP rooms, including the Alice rooms, do this automatically in their tracking.
4. **Let the runner work.** Do not close the last part early out of fear. The runner is what makes the good weeks.

## How wins and losses are counted

Every honest signal service should explain how it counts results. In the Alice VIP rooms:

- A signal that reaches **TP1, TP2, TP3 or more counts as a win**, even if price comes back later. You were paid at the target.
- A signal that hits the stop loss **before any target** counts as a loss.
- A signal that expires without reaching a target or the stop is left out of the win rate.

Each target hit is listed separately (for example "TP1 ✅ 42, TP2 ✅ 31, TP3 ✅ 18") so you can see how far the signals usually travel.

## Entry notes you will see

- **"Enter at or above the level"** on a buy, or **"at or below"** on a sell, means the setup is a breakout. Wait until price trades through the level.
- **"Entry zone"** means any price inside the range is fine. Many traders split the order in two inside the zone.
- **"Leverage 10X"** on crypto futures tells you the leverage the signal was planned with. The percentages are on price, not on your margin, so with 10x leverage a 2% move is about 20% on the margin.

## Common mistakes

- Entering long after the entry, when price is already close to TP1. The risk is the same, the reward is much smaller.
- Moving the stop further away because "it will come back".
- Risking 10% of the account on one signal because "this one looks sure". No signal is sure.
- Ignoring news. A great technical signal can be wiped out by a surprise CPI or NFP number. Check the economic calendar before you enter.

## Frequently asked questions

### What does TP1, TP2 and TP3 mean?

They are take-profit levels. TP1 is the first and closest target, TP2 the second and TP3 the third. Traders usually close part of the position at each level and move the stop loss to the entry price after TP2.

### What does "SL moved to entry" mean?

The stop loss is moved to the price where you opened the trade. If the market turns around, the rest of the position closes at zero instead of a loss. It is also called a break-even stop.

### Is a trade a win if only TP1 was hit?

Yes. Reaching any target means profit was available and many traders banked part of the position there. That is why most signal services, and the Alice VIP rooms, count TP1 and above as a win.

### How much should I risk on one signal?

Most professional traders risk between 0.5% and 2% of their account per trade. Use a position size calculator so the distance to the stop loss equals that amount.
