Gold (XAU/USD) is the favourite market of many forex traders: it trends well, moves fast and reacts strongly to the news. It also punishes traders who enter without a plan. This is a complete **gold trading strategy** you can follow every day to answer the question every gold trader asks in the morning: **XAUUSD buy or sell today?**

## Step 1: Set the daily bias

Open the daily and 4-hour charts and decide the bias before the session starts.

- **Bullish bias:** gold above its rising 50 EMA on the 4-hour chart, making higher lows, and the US dollar weakening.
- **Bearish bias:** gold below a falling 50 EMA, making lower highs, and the dollar strengthening.
- **Neutral:** price stuck between two clear levels. Wait for the break, or trade the edges with small size.

Gold usually moves **opposite to the US dollar and US real yields**. If the dollar index and Treasury yields are falling, gold buyers have the wind at their back.

## Step 2: Mark the levels that matter

Mark these on the chart every morning:

1. **Yesterday's high and low**: the most watched intraday levels.
2. **The Asian session range**: London often breaks one side of it.
3. **Round numbers**: gold respects every 50 and 100 dollar level.
4. **Last week's high and low** and major swing points on the 4-hour chart.

## Step 3: Trade the right session

Gold is quiet during the Asian session and comes alive in **London** and especially the **London and New York overlap**, when US data is released. A simple session plan:

- **Asian session:** mark the range. No trades for most traders.
- **London open:** watch for a break of the Asian range in the direction of your daily bias.
- **New York open:** the biggest moves of the day, especially around 13:30 GMT data releases.

## Step 4: The entry setups

The best gold trading strategy keeps setups simple. Two work very well:

**1. Pullback in the trend.** In a bullish bias, wait for gold to pull back to a support level or the 21 EMA on the 1-hour chart, then buy when a bullish candle closes. Stop below the level, target the day's high and the next resistance.

**2. Breakout and retest.** When gold breaks a key level with a strong close, wait for price to come back and test the level from the other side. Enter on the rejection candle. This filters out many fakeouts.

## Step 5: Stop loss, size and targets

Gold moves 20 to 40 dollars on a normal day and much more on news days. Give your stop loss room: beyond the level, plus a buffer of a few dollars. Then size the position so that hitting the stop costs no more than 1% of the account.

- On most brokers, 1 standard lot of XAU/USD is 100 ounces, so a 1 dollar move is worth 100 dollars per lot.
- A 0.10 lot moves 10 dollars per 1 dollar move in gold.

Targets should be at least **twice the risk**. Take part of the profit at the first target and trail the rest.

## Step 6: Respect the news

Gold reacts strongly to:

- **US CPI inflation** and **PCE**
- **Non-farm payrolls**
- **Fed decisions** and speeches by the Fed chair
- **Geopolitical risk**, which drives safe-haven buying

Check the economic calendar every morning. Many gold traders close or reduce positions before a high-impact release and trade the new direction 15 to 30 minutes after it.

## XAUUSD buy or sell today: a 60-second checklist

1. Daily and 4H trend: up, down or range?
2. Dollar and yields: supporting or fighting the bias?
3. Key levels marked: yesterday's high/low, Asian range, round numbers?
4. High-impact news today? At what time?
5. Setup: pullback or breakout and retest?
6. Stop, size (1% risk) and target (2R or more) written down?

If you cannot answer all six, the best trade today is no trade.

### Why does gold fall when the dollar rises?

Gold is priced in US dollars. A stronger dollar makes gold more expensive for buyers using other currencies, and higher US yields make non-yielding gold less attractive.
