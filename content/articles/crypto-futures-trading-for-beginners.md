**Crypto futures trading** lets you profit when the price goes up (long) or down (short), with leverage that multiplies both gains and losses. It is where most crypto volume happens and where most beginners lose money, usually for the same three reasons: too much leverage, no stop loss and no idea where liquidation sits. This guide fixes all three.

## What are crypto futures?

Most crypto futures are **perpetual futures** (perps): contracts that track the price of a coin such as BTC or ETH and never expire. You do not own the coin. You own a position that gains or loses value as the price moves.

- **Long:** you profit if the price rises.
- **Short:** you profit if the price falls.
- **Margin:** the money you put up for the position.
- **Leverage:** how many times larger the position is than your margin.

With 100 USDT of margin and 10x leverage you control a 1,000 USDT position. A 5% move in your favour makes 50 USDT (50% of your margin). A 5% move against you loses the same 50 USDT.

## Liquidation: the number you must know before every trade

If the market moves against you far enough to wipe out your margin, the exchange **liquidates** the position: it closes it automatically and you lose that margin. The higher the leverage, the closer the liquidation price.

![Liquidation distance for a BTC long at 70,000 with 2x to 50x leverage](img:art/futures_liquidation.webp "The same BTC long at 70,000: at 2x, liquidation is about 50% away; at 50x it is only about 1.5% away.")

A normal Bitcoin day can easily move 2% to 4%. At 50x, that is enough to liquidate you even if your idea was right. That is why professionals in **leverage trading crypto** usually stay between 2x and 10x and use a stop loss well before the liquidation price.

## Isolated vs cross margin

- **Isolated margin:** only the margin of that position can be lost. Best for beginners.
- **Cross margin:** your whole futures balance supports every position. A bad trade can drain the account.

Start with isolated margin and one position at a time.

## Funding rate: the hidden cost of perpetual futures

Perpetual futures use a **funding rate** to stay close to the spot price. Every few hours (often every 8 hours), longs pay shorts when funding is positive, and shorts pay longs when it is negative.

- High positive funding means the market is crowded with longs. It also costs you money to hold a long.
- Very negative funding means shorts are crowded and a short squeeze becomes more likely.

Funding and open interest together are one of the best sentiment tools in crypto. When open interest jumps and funding is extreme, a sharp move against the crowd often follows.

## A simple futures trade plan

1. **Direction:** use the higher timeframe trend. Long only above key support in an uptrend, short only below resistance in a downtrend.
2. **Entry:** at a level, not in the middle of a move. A pullback to support or a breakout retest.
3. **Stop loss:** where the idea is wrong, beyond the level, plus a buffer.
4. **Position size:** risk a fixed 1% of your account. Work out the size from the distance to the stop.
5. **Leverage:** only what you need to reach that size. Leverage should be the result of the plan, not the starting point.
6. **Targets:** the next levels. Take partial profit and move the stop to break-even.

## Long and short crypto: when to do which

**Long and short crypto** trades are equally valid. In a bull market, longs at support work best; in a bear market, shorts at resistance. In a range, both work at the edges. The mistake is fighting the trend: shorting every new high in a strong bull market or buying every dip in a crash.

## Five rules that keep futures traders alive

1. Never risk more than 1% to 2% of the account on one trade.
2. Always place the stop loss when you open the trade.
3. Keep leverage low, and check the liquidation price first.
4. Avoid opening trades minutes before CPI, payrolls or a Fed decision.
5. Keep a trading journal and review your trades every week.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is crypto futures trading good for beginners?

Only with low leverage, isolated margin, small size and a stop loss on every trade. Practise first with a demo account or very small positions.

### What leverage should I use?

Most experienced traders use 2x to 10x. Choose the leverage that gives you the position size your 1% risk rule allows, not the maximum the exchange offers.

### Why was I liquidated when the price came back?

Because the position was closed when the margin ran out. With high leverage, a normal wick can trigger liquidation before the price recovers. Lower leverage and a stop loss beyond the wick zone prevent this.
