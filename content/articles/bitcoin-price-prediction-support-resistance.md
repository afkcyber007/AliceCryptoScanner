"Where is Bitcoin going next?" is one of the most searched questions in finance. Nobody can tell you the exact future price, and anyone who promises a guaranteed **Bitcoin price prediction** is selling something. What professionals do instead is build **scenarios**: if BTC holds this support, the next target is there; if it breaks, the next level is here. This guide shows you how to build that kind of BTC price prediction yourself, in about ten minutes a day.

![BTC/USDT 4-hour chart with support, resistance and the next target](img:art/btc_levels.webp "A BTC 4H example: two supports below, the previous high as resistance, and the next target if it breaks.")

## Step 1: Start with the trend on the daily chart

Every good BTC analysis starts from the top. On the daily chart, ask one question: is Bitcoin making **higher highs and higher lows** (uptrend), **lower highs and lower lows** (downtrend), or moving sideways?

- In an uptrend, your Bitcoin price prediction should lean bullish and you look for **buy zones at support**.
- In a downtrend, lean bearish and look for **sell zones at resistance**.
- In a range, trade from one side to the other, or wait for the breakout.

The 200-day moving average is a useful second filter. Bitcoin above a rising 200-day average has historically been a healthier market than Bitcoin below a falling one.

## Step 2: Mark Bitcoin support and resistance

**Bitcoin support and resistance** levels are prices where buyers or sellers stepped in before. Mark them on the daily and 4-hour charts:

1. **Previous swing highs and lows**: the clearest levels on any chart.
2. **Round numbers**: 60,000, 65,000, 70,000 and so on attract orders and headlines.
3. **Breakout levels**: an old resistance that broke often becomes new support (the "support and resistance flip").
4. **The weekly open and the monthly open**: many large traders use them as reference prices.

Draw levels as **zones**, not thin lines. Bitcoin regularly wicks a few hundred dollars past a level before reversing.

## Step 3: Find the next BTC target

Your **BTC next target** is simply the next zone in the direction of the trade:

- If BTC is bouncing from support, the first target is the nearest resistance above.
- If BTC breaks resistance and closes above it, the next target is the following resistance, or a Fibonacci extension (127.2% or 161.8%) of the last swing when price is at new highs.
- If support breaks, the next level below becomes the target for sellers.

Write it down as a plan: *"Above 64,000 the bias is bullish with targets at 67,500 and 70,000. A 4-hour close below 61,800 cancels this and opens 58,000."* That is a professional Bitcoin price prediction: clear, testable and with an exit if you are wrong.

## Step 4: Confirm with volume, momentum and derivatives

Levels tell you where; confirmation tells you whether the move is real.

- **Volume:** breakouts with rising volume follow through more often. Breakouts on falling volume are often fakeouts.
- **RSI:** RSI above 50 supports bullish scenarios; bearish divergence at resistance warns that buyers are tired.
- **Funding rates and open interest:** very positive funding and fast-rising open interest mean many traders are long with leverage. That fuel can drive a squeeze lower if support breaks.
- **Spot ETF flows:** strong inflows into spot Bitcoin ETFs have become one of the biggest drivers of BTC demand.

## Step 5: Check the news and the calendar

Bitcoin reacts strongly to US data and central banks. Before you trust your BTC price prediction today, check the economic calendar for **CPI inflation, non-farm payrolls and Fed (FOMC) decisions**. A big release can push price through any level within minutes. Crypto-specific news such as ETF decisions, exchange hacks, regulation and large liquidations matters too, which is why the Alice news page marks high-impact headlines.

## Step 6: Plan the trade and the risk

A prediction is only useful with a plan:

- **Entry:** at support in an uptrend, or after a confirmed breakout and retest.
- **Stop loss:** beyond the zone that would prove you wrong, plus a small buffer.
- **Size:** risk 1% or less of your account per trade. Calculate it from the stop, not from how confident you feel.
- **Targets:** the next resistance zones. Many traders take partial profit at the first target and move the stop to break-even.

## Short-term vs long-term Bitcoin forecasts

Short-term BTC analysis (hours to days) depends on levels, momentum and news. A long-term **Bitcoin forecast** (months to years) depends on adoption, ETF demand, liquidity from central banks and the four-year halving cycle. Do not mix them: a long-term bullish view does not stop a 15% drop next week, and a short-term sell setup does not mean the bull market is over.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can anyone predict the Bitcoin price exactly?

No. Professional traders work with scenarios and probabilities: where the trend points, which levels matter and where the idea is wrong. That is more useful than a single number.

### What is the most important Bitcoin support level?

The most recent higher low on the daily chart in an uptrend, or the most recent major low in a downtrend. Round numbers and old breakout levels come next.

### Which timeframe is best for BTC analysis?

Use the daily chart for direction, the 4-hour chart for levels and the 1-hour or 15-minute chart for the entry.

### Does news really move Bitcoin?

Yes. US inflation data, Fed decisions, ETF flows and regulation can move Bitcoin several percent in minutes. Always check the economic calendar before trading.
