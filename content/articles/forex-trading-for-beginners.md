The foreign exchange market is the largest financial market in the world, with trillions of dollars traded every day. It is open 24 hours from Monday to Friday and you can start with a small account. That accessibility is also its danger: it is easy to start and easy to lose money without a plan. This guide gives you the foundations.

![Anatomy of a forex quote: base currency, quote currency, bid, ask and spread](img:art/forex_quote.webp "A forex quote: EUR is the base currency, USD the quote currency. You buy at the ask and sell at the bid; the difference is the spread.")

## What you are actually trading

In forex you always trade one currency against another. In **EUR/USD**:

- **EUR** is the base currency.
- **USD** is the quote currency.
- A price of 1.0850 means one euro costs 1.0850 US dollars.

If you **buy** EUR/USD, you profit if the euro strengthens against the dollar. If you **sell**, you profit if it weakens.

## Majors, crosses and exotics

- **Majors:** pairs with the US dollar such as EUR/USD, GBP/USD, USD/JPY. Tightest spreads, most liquidity.
- **Crosses:** pairs without the dollar, such as EUR/GBP or GBP/JPY.
- **Exotics:** a major currency against an emerging one, such as USD/TRY. Wide spreads and big swings.

Beginners usually do best with two or three major pairs and gold.

## Bid, ask and spread

The **bid** is the price you sell at, the **ask** the price you buy at. The difference is the **spread**, a cost you pay on every trade. The more liquid the pair and the busier the session, the tighter the spread.

## Pips and lots

- A **pip** is the standard unit of price movement: 0.0001 for most pairs, 0.01 for yen pairs.
- A **standard lot** is 100,000 units of the base currency, a mini lot 10,000 and a micro lot 1,000.
- On EUR/USD, one pip is worth about $10 on a standard lot, $1 on a mini lot and $0.10 on a micro lot.

## Leverage and margin

Brokers let you control a large position with a small deposit called margin. With 1:100 leverage, $1,000 of margin controls $100,000. Leverage magnifies gains and losses equally. Your real risk should be decided by your stop loss and position size, not by the leverage your broker offers.

## When to trade

Forex follows the sun through four sessions: Sydney, Tokyo, London and New York. The busiest time is the London–New York overlap, roughly 12:00 to 16:00 UTC.

## What moves currencies

- **Interest rates** and central bank decisions.
- **Inflation** data such as CPI.
- **Employment** data such as US non-farm payrolls.
- **Growth** data such as GDP and PMI.
- **Risk sentiment:** in fearful markets, money flows into the US dollar, Japanese yen and Swiss franc.

## Your first trade plan

1. Pick one pair and one timeframe, such as EUR/USD on the 1-hour chart.
2. Identify the trend on the 4-hour or daily chart.
3. Mark support and resistance.
4. Wait for price to reach a level in the direction of the trend.
5. Enter with a stop loss beyond the level and a target at least twice the risk.
6. Size the position so the loss is no more than 1% of your account.
7. Record the trade in a journal.

## Start on a demo account

Practise on a demo account until you can follow your plan for at least a month of trades. Then move to a small live account. The goal of the first year is to survive and learn, not to get rich.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much money do I need to start forex trading?

You can open an account with a small amount, but a few hundred dollars and micro lots let you risk 1% per trade with sensible stops.

### Is forex trading gambling?

Without a plan and risk management, it behaves like gambling. With a tested strategy, fixed risk and a journal, it becomes a skill-based activity, though still risky.

### Which pair is best for beginners?

EUR/USD is a common choice: tight spreads, deep liquidity and plenty of educational material.
