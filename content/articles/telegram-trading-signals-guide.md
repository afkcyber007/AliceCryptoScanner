Telegram has become the home of trading signals. It is fast, free to use and works on every phone. That also makes it the home of most signal scams. This guide shows how real signal channels work and how to tell a professional service from a group that only wants your deposit.

## Why traders use Telegram for signals

- **Speed.** A message arrives within a second, on your phone and computer at the same time.
- **Simple format.** Pair, direction, entry, stop loss and targets fit in one message you can copy.
- **Updates.** The same chat tells you when TP1 is hit, when to move the stop to entry and when the trade is closed.

## Red flags of a fake signal group

1. **No stop loss.** Real traders always define where they are wrong.
2. **Only wins are posted.** If you never see a loss, losses are being deleted.
3. **Edited messages.** Entries "adjusted" after the move happened are a classic trick. Check the edit marks.
4. **"Send me money and I will trade for you."** Never send funds to a person to "manage". Legitimate services never ask for this.
5. **Guaranteed profits.** Nobody can guarantee profit in forex, gold or crypto.
6. **Pressure to deposit with one unknown broker.** Some groups earn from your losses through hidden broker deals.

## What a trustworthy signal service shows you

- A **full track record**, wins and losses, with dates.
- **Clear rules for counting results**, for example "every signal that reaches TP1 or more is a win, a stop before TP1 is a loss".
- **Entry, stop loss and several targets** in every signal, plus updates when targets are hit.
- **Results tracked on real candles**, not screenshots that can be cropped.

## How to follow a Telegram signal safely

1. Read the whole signal before you act: entry, stop loss and targets.
2. Calculate the position size from the stop loss.
3. Place the stop loss with the order, not later.
4. Take partial profit at TP1 and TP2, and move the stop to entry after TP2.
5. Never add to a losing signal.
